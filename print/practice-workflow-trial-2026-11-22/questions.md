## 1. Check a rational-function analysis - 16 points

Let

$$
F(x)=\frac{x+3}{x-1}.
$$

A classmate writes:

| Feature | Classmate's answer |
|---|---|
| Domain | All real numbers |
| Range | All real numbers |
| Vertical asymptote | y=1 |
| Horizontal asymptote | x=1 |
| x-intercept | (3,0) |
| y-intercept | (0,3) |
| Direction | Increasing everywhere |

Replace every incorrect entry with a correct answer. Give domains, ranges and
increasing/decreasing intervals in interval notation. Support your direction
answer with ordered input-output pairs on each side of the vertical asymptote.

Sketch F with its intercepts and asymptotes labeled. Describe both distant ends
and its transformations from y=1/x. Find all inputs for which F(x)=3, and
check your answer in the original rule.

Graph requirement: both axes -8 to 8, one unit per square.

## 2. Two ways to continue a fundraiser - 14 points

A school club has collected 500 dollars in donations and spent 100 dollars on
printing. It is considering two separate plans for collecting more donations:

- Plan A: use an online service that charges 10% of every additional donation.
- Plan B: collect additional donations directly, with no additional expenses.

Both plans start with the same 500 dollars collected and 100 dollars spent.
Let x be the additional donation amount before any service charge. Include the
printing cost in total expenses. Assume there are no other costs.

For each plan, build a model for total expenses as a fraction of total donations
collected before expenses. State the meaningful domain of each model.

Find the starting expense percentage and the percentage under each plan after
collecting 500 additional dollars.

Can each plan make total expenses exactly 10% of total donations? If so, find
the additional amount needed and check the actual totals. If not, justify why
it is impossible using the model.

## 3. Repair the reverse rule - 12 points

A device follows

$$
f(x)=\frac{3x-4}{2},\qquad -2\le x\le6.
$$

A student proposes the reverse rule

$$
r(y)=\frac{2}{3}y+4.
$$

Decide whether r is the inverse of f. If not, derive the correct inverse.
Give the domain and range of both the forward function and its inverse.

Find the original input that produced output 4. Verify that your corrected
rules undo each other in both orders using a variable.


## 4. Two legitimate inverse functions - 12 points

Let

$$
h(x)=(x-2)^2-3.
$$

Find every input for which h(x)=6. Explain why h, with all real inputs allowed,
does not have an inverse function.

Maya uses h only for x<=2. Leo uses h only for x>=2.
Derive the inverse function for each restricted version, giving its domain and
range. Do their inverse functions return the same value at input 6?

For Maya's version, sketch h and its inverse on the same axes, draw y=x, and
label the vertex/endpoint, both curves' intercepts, and a corresponding pair of
points. Describe the transformations of Maya's inverse from the square-root function.

Graph requirement: both axes -6 to 6, one unit per square.

## 5. A bounded piecewise function - 14 points

P uses these two rules:

$$
P(x)=x+2 \quad \text{for } -4\le x<-1.
$$

$$
P(x)=(x-1)^2 \quad \text{for } -1\le x\le3.
$$

Sketch the complete function with every endpoint marked open or closed.
Find P(-4), P(-1) and P(3), and give the complete domain and range.

Solve P(x)=1. Show which rule produced each candidate and explain why any
candidate must be rejected.

Graph requirement: both axes -6 to 6, one unit per square.

## 6. Read before nesting - 8 points

The graph of f consists of straight segments joining these vertices in order,
with both end vertices included:

$$
(-4,0),\quad(-2,2),\quad(0,-2),\quad(2,0),\quad(4,2).
$$

There are no other inputs in f's domain. Let g(x)=1-x for all real x.

Find all x-intercepts of f. Calculate f(g(3)) and g(f(3)), showing the intermediate
value in each case.

Give the complete domain of f(g(x)). Find every solution of g(f(x))=0.

Graph requirement: the source f graph will be supplied; both axes -6 to 6,
one unit per square. No student reconstruction of the source graph is required.

## 7. Multiplicity, sign and scale - 10 points

A degree-four polynomial has only the zeros -2, 1 and 3. It touches the axis
at -2 and crosses at the other two zeros. Its y-intercept is (0,-12).

Find its factored rule. State its leading coefficient, both distant end behaviors,
and the intervals where its values are positive and negative.

Calculate p(2), then sketch the polynomial with the zeros, y-intercept and
calculated point labeled at the correct scale. Exact turning-point calculations
are not required.

Graph requirement: x from -6 to 6 at one per square; y from -24 to 24 at four
per square. Both axes centered at zero.

## 8. Same values, different functions? - 14 points

Consider

$$
A(x)=\frac{x^3-2x^2+x-2}{x^2-4}
$$

$$
B(x)=\frac{x^2+1}{x+2}.
$$

Decide whether A and B are the same function. Justify your answer algebraically
and give both domains.

Identify every hole and vertical asymptote of A, with the hole's coordinates.
Derive any slant asymptote by division, and describe both distant ends.

Sketch A, labeling its discontinuities, asymptote lines and intercepts.
Explain whether A can cross its slant asymptote.

Graph requirement: x from -6 to 6 at one per square; y from -12 to 12 at two
per square. Both axes centered at zero.
