Graphing Polynomial Functions
1. Graph → equation (8 pts)

Graph shows zeros at \(x = -3, -1, 2\); \(y\)-intercept \((0, 6)\); odd-degree, negative leading coefficient (top-left to bottom-right).

(a) Degree
3 (cubic) — three distinct real zeros, curve changes direction twice.
(b) End behavior
As \(x \to -\infty,\ k(x) \to +\infty\); as \(x \to +\infty,\ k(x) \to -\infty\). Odd degree, negative leading coefficient → top-left, bottom-right.
(c) Domain and Range
Domain: \((-\infty, \infty)\)    Range: \((-\infty, \infty)\)
(d) Increasing / Decreasing (graph estimate)
Increasing on \((-\infty, \approx{-2.1}) \cup (\approx{0.8}, \approx{1.1})\); Decreasing on \((\approx{-2.1}, \approx{0.8})\) and \((\approx{1.1}, \infty)\).
Exact turning pts: \(k'(x)=-3x^2-4x+5=0\) ⇒ \(x \approx -2.12\) (local max) and \(x \approx 0.79\) (local min). Accept graph-read estimates.
(e) Relative max / min (estimate)
Relative max near \(x \approx -2.1\) (\(k \approx 7.2\));   Relative min near \(x \approx 0.8\) (\(k \approx 4.1\)).
(f) Factored form given \(k(1) = 8\)
Zeros at \(-3, -1, 2\) ⇒ \(k(x) = a(x+3)(x+1)(x-2)\).
Plug \(x = 1\): \(k(1) = a(4)(2)(-1) = -8a = 8 \Rightarrow a = -1.\)
\(k(x) = -(x+3)(x+1)(x-2)\)
(g) Standard form
Expand inner pair: \((x+3)(x+1) = x^2 + 4x + 3.\)
Then \((x^2+4x+3)(x-2) = x^3 - 2x^2 + 4x^2 - 8x + 3x - 6 = x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x - 6.\)
Apply \(a = -1\): \(k(x) = -x^3 - 2x^2 + 5x + 6.\)
\(k(x) = -x^3 - 2x^2 + 5x + 6\)
2. \(f(x) = -x^2(x-3)(x+1)\) (8 pts)
(a) Degree
4 — \(x^2 \cdot x \cdot x = x^4\) when fully expanded.
(b) End behavior
As \(x \to \pm\infty,\ f(x) \to -\infty\) (both ends DOWN). Even degree with negative leading coefficient → both ends fall.
(c) Sketch
Zeros: \(x = 0\) (mult 2, bounces), \(x = 3\) (mult 1, crosses), \(x = -1\) (mult 1, crosses).
\(y\)-intercept: \(f(0) = 0\). Both ends down. Graph crosses at \(x = -1\), bounces at origin, crosses at \(x = 3\), both ends fall to \(-\infty\).
(d) Standard form
First: \((x-3)(x+1) = x^2 - 2x - 3.\)
Then: \(-x^2(x^2 - 2x - 3) = -(x^4 - 2x^3 - 3x^2) = -x^4 + 2x^3 + 3x^2.\)
\(f(x) = -x^4 + 2x^3 + 3x^2\)

Part 2 — Calculator Allowed
Factoring Polynomials
4. Factor completely: \(4x^3 - 8x^2 - 60x\) (4 pts)
GCF = \(4x\). \(4x(x^2 - 2x - 15) = 4x(x-5)(x+3).\)
\(4x(x-5)(x+3)\)
Watch-out (from Quiz 2): Don't forget the GCF step before the trinomial factor. Always pull GCF first.
5. Factor by grouping: \(x^3 - 3x^2 - 4x + 12\) (4 pts)
Group: \((x^3 - 3x^2) + (-4x + 12) = x^2(x - 3) - 4(x - 3) = (x-3)(x^2 - 4).\)
Difference of squares: \((x-3)(x-2)(x+2).\)
\((x-3)(x-2)(x+2)\)
6. Factor completely: \(16x^4 - 81\) (4 pts)
Difference of squares: \(16x^4 - 81 = (4x^2)^2 - 9^2 = (4x^2 - 9)(4x^2 + 9).\)
Factor \(4x^2 - 9 = (2x-3)(2x+3).\) \(4x^2 + 9\) is irreducible over the reals.
\((2x-3)(2x+3)(4x^2 + 9)\)
7. Factor: \(6x^2 + 17x - 14\) (4 pts)
AC method: \(6 \cdot (-14) = -84\). Need two numbers that multiply to \(-84\) and add to \(17\): \(21\) and \(-4.\)
Split middle: \(6x^2 + 21x - 4x - 14 = 3x(2x + 7) - 2(2x + 7) = (2x + 7)(3x - 2).\)
\((2x + 7)(3x - 2)\)
Dividing Polynomials
8. \((x^3 - 4x^2 + 5x - 2) \div (x - 1)\) — long division (4 pts)
Step 1: \(x^3 \div x = x^2\); multiply, subtract → \(-3x^2 + 5x - 2.\)
Step 2: \(-3x^2 \div x = -3x\); multiply, subtract → \(2x - 2.\)
Step 3: \(2x \div x = 2\); multiply, subtract → \(0.\)
Quotient: \(x^2 - 3x + 2\)  ·  Remainder: 0
9. \((2x^3 + x^2 - 5x + 3) \div (x + 2)\) — long division (4 pts)
\(2x^3 \div x = 2x^2\); subtract → \(-3x^2 - 5x + 3.\)
\(-3x^2 \div x = -3x\); subtract → \(x + 3.\)
\(x \div x = 1\); subtract → remainder \(1.\)
Quotient: \(2x^2 - 3x + 1\)  ·  Remainder: 1
10. \((x^4 - 2x^3 - 7x^2 + 8x + 12) \div (x - 3)\) — synthetic (4 pts)
Synthetic with \(c = 3\): coefficients \([1, -2, -7, 8, 12].\)
Carry down 1. \(1 \cdot 3 = 3\); \(-2 + 3 = 1.\) \(1 \cdot 3 = 3\); \(-7 + 3 = -4.\)
\(-4 \cdot 3 = -12\); \(8 + (-12) = -4.\) \(-4 \cdot 3 = -12\); \(12 + (-12) = 0.\)
Quotient: \(x^3 + x^2 - 4x - 4\)  ·  Remainder: 0
11. \((x^4 + 3x^3 - x + 5) \div (x + 4)\) — synthetic (4 pts)
Watch-out: The \(x^2\) term is missing — you must include a \(0\) placeholder. Coefficients: \([1, 3, 0, -1, 5].\)
Synthetic with \(c = -4\). Carry 1. \(1 \cdot -4 = -4\); \(3 + (-4) = -1.\)
\(-1 \cdot -4 = 4\); \(0 + 4 = 4.\) \(4 \cdot -4 = -16\); \(-1 + (-16) = -17.\)
\(-17 \cdot -4 = 68\); \(5 + 68 = 73.\)
Quotient: \(x^3 - x^2 + 4x - 17\)  ·  Remainder: 73
Manipulating Polynomial Expressions
12. Expand \(2x(x - 3)(x + 5)\) (4 pts)
Inner two first: \((x - 3)(x + 5) = x^2 + 2x - 15.\)
Multiply by \(2x\): \(2x(x^2 + 2x - 15) = 2x^3 + 4x^2 - 30x.\)
\(2x^3 + 4x^2 - 30x\)
Watch-out (from Quiz 1 Q1d): Multiply two binomials first, then distribute the monomial. Don't try to do all three at once.
13. \((3x^2 - 5x + 2) - (x^2 + 4x - 7)\) (4 pts)
Distribute the negative: \(3x^2 - 5x + 2 - x^2 - 4x + 7.\)
Combine: \(2x^2 - 9x + 9.\)
\(2x^2 - 9x + 9\)
Watch-out (from Assessment 2A Q10): The negative must hit EVERY term in the second polynomial — including the \(-7\), which becomes \(+7\).
14. Simplify \(\dfrac{x^3 - 9x}{x^2 - 2x - 3}\) (4 pts)
Factor numerator: \(x(x^2 - 9) = x(x-3)(x+3).\)
Factor denominator: \(x^2 - 2x - 3 = (x-3)(x+1).\)
Cancel common factor \((x-3)\): result is \(\dfrac{x(x+3)}{x+1}\) (with \(x \ne 3\)).
\(\dfrac{x(x+3)}{x+1}\)
CRITICAL watch-out (from Assessment 2A Q13): You must FACTOR before cancelling. Do NOT cancel individual terms like "\(x^3 \to x\)" — that is mathematically wrong. Only common factors cancel.
15. Expand \((2x - 1)(x + 3)^2\) in standard form (4 pts)
\((x+3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9.\)
\((2x - 1)(x^2 + 6x + 9) = 2x \cdot (x^2 + 6x + 9) + (-1) \cdot (x^2 + 6x + 9)\)
\(= 2x^3 + 12x^2 + 18x - x^2 - 6x - 9 = 2x^3 + 11x^2 + 12x - 9.\)
\(2x^3 + 11x^2 + 12x - 9\)
Modeling
16. Snowboard rental — linear (5 pts)
(a)
\(C(d) = 25 + 18d\) dollars   (setup fee + per-day rate)
(b) 7 days
\(C(7) = 25 + 18(7) = 25 + 126 = 151.\)
$151
(c) Days affordable on $169
\(169 \ge 25 + 18d \Rightarrow 144 \ge 18d \Rightarrow d \le 8.\)
8 full days   (at exactly $169)
17. Sneaker revenue — quadratic (5 pts)
(a) Price and quantity
Price = \(20 + x\) dollars/pair  ·  Quantity = \(200 - 5x\) pairs
(b) \(R(x)\) in standard form
\(R(x) = (20 + x)(200 - 5x) = 4000 - 100x + 200x - 5x^2 = -5x^2 + 100x + 4000.\)
\(R(x) = -5x^2 + 100x + 4000\)
(c) \(x\) that maximizes
Vertex: \(x = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = -\dfrac{100}{2(-5)} = -\dfrac{100}{-10} = 10.\)
\(x = 10\)   (10 price increases of $1 each)
(d) Max revenue and price
\(R(10) = -5(100) + 100(10) + 4000 = -500 + 1000 + 4000 = 4500.\) Price = \(20 + 10 = 30\), Quantity = \(200 - 50 = 150\). Check: \(30 \cdot 150 = 4500\). ✓
Max revenue = $4,500/month  ·  Price = $30/pair
18. Car depreciation — exponential (5 pts)
(a) Model
\(V(t) = 18{,}500 \cdot (0.88)^t\)   (12% loss means 88% remains each year)
(b) Value in 2026 (t = 6)
\(V(6) = 18{,}500 \cdot (0.88)^6 = 18{,}500 \cdot 0.4644 \approx 8{,}591.\)
$8,591 (approximately)
(c) Year when value first drops below $8,000
Solve \(18{,}500 \cdot (0.88)^t = 8{,}000.\)
\((0.88)^t = \dfrac{8000}{18500} = 0.4324\ldots\)
Take log of both sides: \(t \cdot \log(0.88) = \log(0.4324).\)
\(t = \dfrac{\log(0.4324)}{\log(0.88)} = \dfrac{-0.3641}{-0.0555} \approx 6.56.\)
So \(t = 7\) full years → year \(2020 + 7 = 2027.\)
2027   (first year value < $8,000)
Watch-out (from Assessment 3 Q5d): When taking a root or solving with logs on the calculator, double-check by plugging your answer back in. The exponent in \(\log(a)/\log(b)\) is easy to mis-key.
Pre-exam reminders for Kai (from prior tests):
  1. Always label answers with units and what they represent (dollars, pairs, years).
  2. Distribute negatives carefully when subtracting polynomials — the minus sign hits every term.
  3. Factor before cancelling. Never cancel individual terms in a rational expression.
  4. GCF first before any other factoring move.
  5. Placeholder zeros for missing terms in synthetic division.
  6. Verify calculator entries for log and root operations.
