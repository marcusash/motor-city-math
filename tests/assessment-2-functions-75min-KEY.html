Prep Test 2: Answer Key and Rubric

75 minutes | 100 points | Keep separate from the student test.

Give follow-through credit: deduct an originating error once, not again for consistent later execution. Score missing parts separately. Do not require unrequested explanations or exact polynomial extrema.

1. Finish a piecewise graph (10 points)

  1. For -2 use 2x+4: p(-2)=0. At -1 and 2 use the constant branch: both values are 3.
  2. Draw y=2x+4 to the left of an open (-1,2). Draw y=3 to the right of a closed (-1,3). The horizontal branch includes its boundary.
  3. Every real x belongs to a branch, so the domain is all real numbers. The left branch gives every y below 2; the right branch adds only 3. Range: (-infinity,2) union {3}.

Scoring

  • 3 points: Three evaluations, 1 each.
  • 2 points: Correct branch shapes and sides, 1 each.
  • 2 points: Boundary coordinates and open/closed marks, 1 per boundary.
  • 3 points: Domain 1; complete range including its gap 2.

2. Transform both coordinates of a given graph (12 points)

  1. For source point (u,v), the new point is (u+2,1-v): shift right 2, reflect across the x-axis, then shift up 1.
  2. The vertices become (-1,0), (1,3), (4,1), and (6,-1). Join in order with straight segments and included endpoints.
  3. Domain [-1,6]. The original range [-2,2] becomes [-1,3] after reflection and vertical shift.

Scoring

  • 4 points: Four transformed vertices, 1 each.
  • 2 points: Correctly connected straight segments. Preserve follow-through credit from an earlier coordinate error.
  • 2 points: All coordinate labels 1; both included endpoints 1.
  • 2 points: Domain.
  • 2 points: Range.

3. Graph both composition orders with a reflection (14 points)

  1. For source (u,v), solve 1-(3/5)x=u, so f(g(x)) maps to ((5/3)(1-u),v). Left-to-right vertices: (-5,2), (-5/3,0), (10/3,3), (20/3,-1). Domain [-5,20/3].
  2. For g(f(x)), map (u,v) to (u,1-(3/5)v). Vertices: (-3,8/5), (-1,-4/5), (2,1), (4,-1/5). Domain [-3,4].
  3. Connect each vertex set in its own left-to-right order. Use solid and dashed lines as specified and mark all endpoints included.
  4. For f(g(x)), reflect horizontally, stretch horizontally by 5/3, then shift right 5/3. For g(f(x)), reflect vertically, compress vertically by 3/5, then shift up 1. Equivalent correctly ordered descriptions are accepted.

Scoring

  • 2 points: Valid coordinate work for both composition orders, 1 each. Accept mapping rules, substitution equations, or equivalent calculations; no particular method is required.
  • 6 points: Four transformed vertices for each composition, 0.75 each.
  • 3 points: Correct segment connections and included endpoints 2; all coordinate labels, graph names, and specified line styles 1 combined.
  • 2 points: Domains, 1 each.
  • 1 points: Correct transformation description for each composition, 0.5 each.

4. Simplify and solve a rational expression (10 points)

  1. Factor the numerator as (x-2)(x+1) and denominator as (x-2)(x+3). Simplify to (x+1)/(x+3), retaining x!=2 and x!=-3. Domain: (-infinity,-3) union (-3,2) union (2,infinity).
  2. Solve 2(x+1)=x+3 to get x=1. It is in the original domain and gives R(1)=1/2.
  3. S excludes only -3. R also excludes 2, where its original denominator is zero. Thus the domains differ, even though their values agree wherever R is defined.

Scoring

  • 5 points: Factoring 2 (1 per polynomial); simplification 1; original domain 2 (1 per exclusion). Accept equivalent domain notation.
  • 3 points: Valid equation work 2; solution x=1, 1. Preserve valid algebraic follow-through from an earlier error.
  • 2 points: Correct domain comparison 1; explains original denominator exclusion at x=2, 1. Do not deduct again for the same restriction error from part (a).

5. Compare two algebraic compositions (6 points)

  1. A(B(x))=(2x-1)^2-2(2x-1)=4x^2-8x+3.
  2. B(A(x))=2(x^2-2x)-1=2x^2-4x-1.
  3. Equality requires 2x^2-4x+4=0, or (x-1)^2+1=0. A real square cannot equal -1, so there are no real solutions. Equivalently, the quadratic discriminant is negative.
  4. The difference A(B(x))-B(A(x))=2(x-1)^2+2 is always positive, so the first composition is always greater. This observation is a valid justification, not an extra student requirement.

Scoring

  • 2 points: A(B(x)): full substitution 1; correct expansion 1.
  • 2 points: B(A(x)): full substitution 1; correct simplification 1.
  • 1 points: Correct equality equation and valid real-solution reasoning. Preserve algebraic follow-through from one earlier error.
  • 1 points: Explicitly concludes no real solutions.

6. Show a flattened crossing in a fifth-degree polynomial (14 points)

  1. P(x)=a(x+2)^2(x-1)^3. From P(0)=-8, -4a=-8, so a=2.
  2. P(x)=2(x+2)^2(x-1)^3 has positive leading coefficient and odd degree: left end down, right end up. Touch the axis at (-2,0) without changing sign; cross with horizontal flattening at (1,0). Label (0,-8).
  3. The squared factor is positive except at -2; the cubic factor is negative below 1 and positive above 1. P>0 on (1,infinity). P<0 on (-infinity,-2) union (-2,1). Exclude both zeros from strict sign intervals.

Scoring

  • 4 points: Multiplicity factors 2; leading-factor calculation and final equation 2.
  • 1 points: Correct labeled y-intercept.
  • 3 points: Both labeled zeros 1; touch at -2 1; clearly flattened crossing at 1 1.
  • 2 points: Both end directions, 1 each.
  • 4 points: Positive interval 1; negative intervals 2; explanation of sign change/no change 1.

7. Keep all restrictions in a composed quotient (8 points)

  1. H(x)=sqrt(9-x^2)/(4-x^2).
  2. The square root requires 9-x^2>=0, giving -3<=x<=3. The denominator requires 4-x^2!=0, so exclude -2 and 2.
  3. Domain [-3,-2) union (-2,2) union (2,3]. At 3, f(g(3))=sqrt(0)=0 and the denominator is -5, so it is allowed. At 2, g(2)=5, so the original denominator is zero and it is not allowed.

Scoring

  • 2 points: Correct substitution in numerator 1 and denominator 1.
  • 2 points: Radical inequality and bounded restriction.
  • 1 points: Both denominator exclusions, 0.5 each.
  • 2 points: Final interval intersection including the allowed endpoints.
  • 1 points: Boundary checks and explanations, 0.5 each.

8. Sketch a quartic with crossings and a touch (8 points)

  1. Degree 4, leading coefficient -1. Zeros -2 and 3 are simple; 1 has multiplicity 2. R(0)=-(2)(1)(-3)=6, so label (0,6).
  2. Both ends fall. Cross upward at (-2,0), stay above the axis, touch and turn at (1,0), stay above until crossing downward at (3,0). Draw a smooth graph with a peak on each side of the touch; exact peaks are not requested.
  3. R>0 on (-2,1) union (1,3); R<0 on (-infinity,-2) union (3,infinity). The even root does not change sign but is excluded from the strict-positive intervals.
  4. Even degree makes the signs at the two far ends the same; the negative leading coefficient makes both ends fall. Odd degree makes the two ends opposite.

Scoring

  • 1 points: Degree/leading coefficient 0.5; zeros and multiplicities 0.5.
  • 1 points: Calculated and labeled y-intercept.
  • 2 points: Labeled zeros 0.5; crossing/touch behavior 1; correctly signed smooth connections 0.5.
  • 1 points: Both ends down.
  • 2 points: Complete positive and negative interval lists, 1 each.
  • 1 points: Even versus odd degree end-behavior explanation.

9. Use a composite model to meet a budget (10 points)

  1. Use C(A(w)): A converts width in feet to area in square feet; C converts area to dollars.
  2. A(w)=12w-w^2=36-(w-6)^2. On [2,6], area increases from 20 to 36. A=30 gives (w-6)^2=6, so the relevant breakpoint is b=6-sqrt(6), approximately 3.55.
  3. Cost is 96w-8w^2 for 2≤w≤62\le w\le62≤w≤6-sqrt(6), and 60w-5w^2+90 for 6-sqrt(6)<w<=6. Domain [2,6]. Either formula gives $240 at the breakpoint; respect the original price condition when assigning the boundary.
  4. At w=4, area is 32, so use the second price branch: C(32)=240+5(2)=$250.
  5. $260 is above the $240 breakpoint price, so the maximum uses the second branch: 5A+90=260 gives A=34. Then 36-(w-6)^2=34 gives w=6 plus or minus sqrt(2). Only 6-sqrt(2) is in [2,6].
  6. The greatest width is 6-sqrt(2) feet, approximately 4.59 feet. Area and both price branches are increasing over the permitted widths, so wider permitted beds exceed the budget. Feasible widths are [2,6-sqrt(2)].

Scoring

  • 2 points: Correct composition 1; correct unit/order explanation 1.
  • 3 points: Width breakpoint 1; two cost expressions 0.5 each; branch intervals and model domain 1.
  • 2 points: Area 32 and correct price branch 1; cost $250 1.
  • 3 points: Budget equation/area 0.5; exact width 1; rounded width 0.5; branch/domain check 0.5; explains why it is the greatest width 0.5. Preserve follow-through from one earlier model error.

10. Construct and graph a square-root function (8 points)

  1. The domain gives h=1. The upper range boundary gives k=3, and the function is reflected downward. Substitute (5,-1): -1=2a+3, so a=-2. Rule r(x)=-2sqrt(x-1)+3.
  2. Starting point (1,3). Three useful additional points are (2,1), (5,-1), (10,-3). Draw a decreasing square-root curve, with included start and a rightward continuation.
  3. Solve -2sqrt(x-1)+3=0: sqrt(x-1)=3/2, x-1=9/4, x=13/4. The x-intercept is (13/4,0). The function decreases on (1,infinity); domain-based endpoint-inclusive wording is also acceptable.

Scoring

  • 3 points: Correct h and k 1; solves for a 1; final rule 1.
  • 3 points: Included labeled starting point 1; three additional labeled points 1; correct connected curve and continuation 1.
  • 2 points: Exact x-intercept 1; decreasing interval 1. Accept (1,infinity) or an explicitly stated decrease over its full domain [1,infinity).
