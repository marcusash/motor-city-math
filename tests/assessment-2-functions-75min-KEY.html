Prep Test 2: Answer Key and Rubric

75 minutes | 100 points | Keep separate from the student test.

Give follow-through credit: deduct an originating error once, not again for consistent later execution. Score missing parts separately. Do not require unrequested explanations or exact polynomial extrema.

1. Finish a piecewise graph (12 points)

  1. For -2 use 2x+4: p(-2)=0. At -1 and 2 use the constant branch: both values are 3.
  2. Draw y=2x+4 to the left of an open (-1,2). Draw y=3 to the right of a closed (-1,3). The horizontal branch includes its boundary.
  3. Every real x belongs to a branch, so the domain is all real numbers. The left branch gives every y below 2; the right branch adds only 3. Range: (-infinity,2) union {3}.

Scoring

  • 3 points: Three evaluations, 1 each.
  • 4 points: Correct branch shapes and sides, 2 each.
  • 2 points: Boundary coordinates and open/closed marks, 1 per boundary.
  • 3 points: Domain 1; complete range including its gap 2.

2. Transform both coordinates of a given graph (12 points)

  1. For source point (u,v), the new point is (u+2,1-v): shift right 2, reflect across the x-axis, then shift up 1.
  2. The vertices become (-1,0), (1,3), (4,1), and (6,-1). Join in order with straight segments and included endpoints.
  3. Domain [-1,6]. The original range [-2,2] becomes [-1,3] after reflection and vertical shift.

Scoring

  • 4 points: Four transformed vertices, 1 each.
  • 2 points: Correctly connected straight segments. Preserve follow-through credit from an earlier coordinate error.
  • 2 points: All coordinate labels 1; both included endpoints 1.
  • 2 points: Domain.
  • 2 points: Range.

3. Graph both composition orders with a reflection (18 points)

  1. For source (u,v), solve 1-2x=u, so f(g(x)) maps to ((1-u)/2,v). Left-to-right vertices: (-3/2,2), (-1/2,0), (1,3), (2,-1). Domain [-3/2,2].
  2. For g(f(x)), map (u,v) to (u,1-2v). Vertices: (-3,3), (-1,-5), (2,1), (4,-3). Domain [-3,4].
  3. Connect each vertex set in its own left-to-right order. Use solid and dashed lines as specified and mark all endpoints included.
  4. For f(g(x)), reflect horizontally, compress horizontally by 1/2, then shift right 1/2. For g(f(x)), reflect vertically, stretch vertically by 2, then shift up 1. Equivalent correctly ordered descriptions are accepted.

Scoring

  • 4 points: Two mapping rules, 2 each.
  • 8 points: Four transformed vertices for each composition, 1 each.
  • 3 points: Correct segment connections and included endpoints 2; all coordinate labels, graph names, and specified line styles 1 combined.
  • 2 points: Domains, 1 each.
  • 1 points: Correct transformation description for each composition, 0.5 each.

4. Evaluate and solve a composition from two graphs (12 points)

  1. The first segment of f has rule y=-2x-6, so f(-3.5)=1. Then g(f(-3.5))=g(1)=2. Also g(-3.5) is undefined because g has domain [-3,3], so f(g(-3.5)) is undefined.
  2. To solve g(f(x))=2, the graph of g gives inner values -1 and 1. Solve f(x)=-1 or f(x)=1 on every segment.
  3. For f(x)=-1, the solutions are -2.5, -1, and 3. For f(x)=1, the solutions are -3.5 and 1. The complete set is {-3.5,-2.5,-1,1,3}. All five inner values are in the domain of g.
  4. The first segment of f has rule y=-2x-6, giving zero -3. The middle segment gives zero 0, and the last gives zero 2.
  5. f increases on (-2,1) and decreases on (-4,-2) union (1,4). Turning-point x-coordinates, not the zeros, are interval boundaries.

Scoring

  • 4 points: First nested evaluation: intermediate value and final value, 1 each. Second: recognizes the inside function is undefined and concludes the composite is undefined, 1 each. Preserve valid follow-through credit.
  • 4 points: Identifies both inner target values, 1; five correct x-values, 0.5 each; complete list with no extras, 0.5. Do not deduct repeatedly for one propagated graph-read error.
  • 2 points: All three zeros: 0.5 each, plus 0.5 for a complete list with no extra zeros.
  • 2 points: Increasing interval 1; two decreasing intervals 0.5 each. Award half of the interval credit when direction and one endpoint are correct.

5. Build and draw a graph from its characteristics (10 points)

  1. The vertex gives h=2, k=4. Substitute (0,0): 0=2a+4, so a=-2. Thus q(x)=-2|x-2|+4.
  2. The graph is a downward V with vertex (2,4), x-intercepts (0,0), (4,0), and rays extending both ways.
  3. Increasing (-infinity,2); decreasing (2,infinity). Domain all real numbers; range (-infinity,4].

Scoring

  • 3 points: Correct form from vertex 1; intercept calculation 1; final rule 1.
  • 4 points: Downward V and continuing rays 1; vertex and both intercept labels, 1 each.
  • 1 points: Increasing/decreasing intervals, 0.5 each.
  • 2 points: Domain/range, 1 each.

6. Show a flattened crossing in a fifth-degree polynomial (14 points)

  1. P(x)=a(x+2)^2(x-1)^3. From P(0)=-8, -4a=-8, so a=2.
  2. P(x)=2(x+2)^2(x-1)^3 has positive leading coefficient and odd degree: left end down, right end up. Touch the axis at (-2,0) without changing sign; cross with horizontal flattening at (1,0). Label (0,-8).
  3. The squared factor is positive except at -2; the cubic factor is negative below 1 and positive above 1. P>0 on (1,infinity). P<0 on (-infinity,-2) union (-2,1). Exclude both zeros from strict sign intervals.

Scoring

  • 4 points: Multiplicity factors 2; leading-factor calculation and final equation 2.
  • 1 points: Correct labeled y-intercept.
  • 3 points: Both labeled zeros 1; touch at -2 1; clearly flattened crossing at 1 1.
  • 2 points: Both end directions, 1 each.
  • 4 points: Positive interval 1; negative intervals 2; explanation of sign change/no change 1.

7. Keep all restrictions in a composed quotient (12 points)

  1. H(x)=sqrt(9-x^2)/(4-x^2).
  2. The square root requires 9-x^2>=0, giving -3<=x<=3. The denominator requires 4-x^2!=0, so exclude -2 and 2.
  3. Domain [-3,-2) union (-2,2) union (2,3]. At 3, f(g(3))=sqrt(0)=0 and the denominator is -5, so it is allowed. At 2, g(2)=5, so the original denominator is zero and it is not allowed.

Scoring

  • 3 points: Correct substitution in numerator 2 and denominator 1.
  • 3 points: Radical inequality and bounded restriction.
  • 2 points: Both denominator exclusions, 1 each.
  • 2 points: Final interval intersection including the allowed endpoints.
  • 2 points: Boundary checks and explanations, 1 each.

8. Sketch a quartic with crossings and a touch (10 points)

  1. Degree 4, leading coefficient -1. Zeros -2 and 3 are simple; 1 has multiplicity 2. R(0)=-(2)(1)(-3)=6, so label (0,6).
  2. Both ends fall. Cross upward at (-2,0), stay above the axis, touch and turn at (1,0), stay above until crossing downward at (3,0). Draw a smooth graph with a peak on each side of the touch; exact peaks are not requested.
  3. R>0 on (-2,1) union (1,3); R<0 on (-infinity,-2) union (3,infinity). The even root does not change sign but is excluded from the strict-positive intervals.
  4. Even degree makes the signs at the two far ends the same; the negative leading coefficient makes both ends fall. Odd degree makes the two ends opposite.

Scoring

  • 2 points: Degree/leading coefficient 1; zeros and multiplicities 1.
  • 1 points: Calculated and labeled y-intercept.
  • 3 points: Labeled zeros 1; crossing/touch behavior 1; correctly signed smooth connections 1.
  • 1 points: Both ends down.
  • 2 points: Complete positive and negative interval lists, 1 each.
  • 1 points: Even versus odd degree end-behavior explanation.
