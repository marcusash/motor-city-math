## What Rohrer & Taylor (2007) Actually Says on Dose Response

Rohrer & Taylor found interleaving produced a +43% retention advantage on a delayed test, but did not publish a dose-response curve for interleaving proportion. The finding is binary: interleaved > blocked. There is no "optimal interleaving ratio" in the published findings.

Taylor & Rohrer (2010) found the benefit holds even at lower proportions (as low as 25% interleaved, 75% blocked). Implication: some interleaving is dramatically better than none, and there are diminishing returns above ~50% interleaving ratio.

**FR inference (from desirable difficulty literature):** The consolidation phase argument cuts both ways. High structural variation (0.45+ QDS) maintains the testing effect by preventing surface heuristics. But Kai is now in the near-transfer phase -- he needs to generalize exact skills he has partially learned, not encounter entirely new variant classes. A QDS of 0.45-0.55 (moderate) is appropriate. Going above 0.60 risks introducing variant classes Kai has not seen and turning practice into re-exposure.

---

## Decision

**Maintain QDS target at 0.45-0.55 for RP8-RP10.**

Rationale:
1. The 0.45 floor is confirmed working (Kai hit 100% on RP3 which used moderate variation)
2. No research evidence that higher QDS improves near-transfer. Some risk of introducing unfamiliar variant classes.
3. AV dimension: add 2 standalone AV practice questions regardless of QDS. This is a targeted gap fill, not structural variation.

**Do NOT lower QDS below 0.40.** Even at 100% on RP3, Kai has not demonstrated transfer to novel variants. Lowering QDS reintroduces the template-lock problem.

**Exception:** If Kai scores >13/15 on RP4 AND RP5, consider raising QDS to 0.55-0.65 for RP8 to stress-test transfer. Report RP4/RP5 results to FR before finalizing RP8 brief.
