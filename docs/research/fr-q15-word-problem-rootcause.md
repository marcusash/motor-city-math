# Root Cause: Kai's Q15 Word Problem Miss

**Author:** FR (Research Lead, Forge)
**Date:** 2026-02-22
**Decision this enables:** GR knows whether Q15 is fair, what the L3 gap is, and how to design RP8 Q15 variants.

---

## The Miss

MVP Q15 (real exam, Feb 18): Word problem requiring exponential growth/decay modeling. Kai skipped Q14 and missed Q15. Score: 0/1.

---

## Evidence Chain

**Evidence 1 (direct observation):** Kai's exam showed Q14 and Q15 blank/wrong. Q15 required: (1) model identification (exponential growth/decay), (2) equation construction from word problem context, (3) solve for unknown using the model.

**Evidence 2 (FR W2.b root cause doc):** The highest-frequency miss on RP1-RP2 was W2.b (parent function identification). Q15 word problems require model identification as step 1. If Kai cannot reliably identify parent function from visual inspection, he cannot reliably identify model from word problem context. Same cognitive gap, different presentation form.

**Evidence 3 (question fairness check):** FR reviewed RP1-RP2 Q15 variants and found 4 of 6 variants used the same "triples/doubles every N hours" form. This is a template lock: Kai could memorize "triples/doubles = exponential" without understanding why. The question is fair when vocabulary varies. It becomes trivially memorizable when the same stem is reused.

**Evidence 4 (cognitive load):** Q15 requires calculating 3^5 = 243 as part of solving. For a student with ADHD managing cognitive load across 15 questions, multi-step arithmetic in a word problem at the end of the exam hits at maximum fatigue.

---

## Root Cause: Two Factors

**Factor 1 (primary): L3 model-writing gap.** Kai can identify exponential functions from a graph or equation (L1-L2). He cannot reliably construct an exponential model from word problem context (L3). RP3's 100% score was on L1-L2 questions. Q15 requires L3.

**Factor 2 (secondary): Cognitive load.** Multi-step word problem + exponential calculation at the end of a 15-question exam hits Kai at maximum fatigue. The cognitive load of model-writing + 3^5 arithmetic is disproportionate to other questions on the exam.

---

## Is the Question Fair?

Yes, if vocabulary is varied. No, if the stem is reused ("triples every N hours" appeared in 4 of 6 RP1-RP2 variants). The math is appropriate for Kai's level. The cognitive load is high but not unreasonable for Algebra II. The fairness issue is vocabulary template lock, not question type.

---

## Learning Prescription

1. **L3 model-writing practice:** Given a word problem description, write the equation (do NOT solve it). 3 practice items in RP4 before Q15. Isolate the model-writing skill from the solve step.
2. **RP8 Q15 vocabulary rotation:** Rotate among "half-life," "compound interest," "bacterial count," "radioactive decay" -- NOT just "triples/doubles." Keep math identical. Test vocabulary transfer, not number recall.
3. **Scratch paper signal:** If Kai uses scratch paper for 3^5, cognitive load is being managed correctly. No intervention needed on arithmetic dimension.

---

*FR confidence: HIGH on Factor 1 (L3 gap). MEDIUM on Factor 2 (cognitive load is inferred from timing context, not directly measured). Two independent evidence paths (W2.b root cause + Q15 variant analysis) converge on the L3 gap as primary.*
