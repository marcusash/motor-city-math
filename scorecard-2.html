Fill Design — What Was Tried, What's In, What Could Come Next
Diagonal Gradient Fill
Current — In Mockup

Gradient runs from bottom-left (x=50,y=108) to top-right (x=540,y=22) in userSpaceOnUse coordinates. Opacity: 1% → 10% → 24%.

Why it works: the gradient intensifies in the same direction as the line climbs — both spatially (bigger fill area) and chromatically (brighter fill). Triple signal: line position + fill area + fill brightness all encoding "you're improving."

Green Bloom at Achievement
Unexplored — Worth Trying

Keep the diagonal white fill but add a very subtle green tint (2-3% opacity rgba(63,185,80)) in the upper-right zone where the line crosses into A territory. The fill would literally turn a trace of green where Kai achieves Grade A.

Risk: could feel noisy if the tint reads as a separate color rather than a subtle achievement glow. Would need to be extremely subtle — maybe 0-0% left → 0-4% green right, layered under the white fill.

Fill Above A Line (Gap Fill)
Unexplored — Bold Move

Instead of filling below the curve, show a subtle fill ABOVE the A grade line in the left portion of the chart (where Kai hasn't reached A yet). This "gap to close" fill fades out as the line approaches and crosses A.

Semantic shift: now the fill shows what Kai needs to earn rather than what he's done. Riskier UX but potentially more motivating for a 14-year-old. Requires careful opacity — can't feel like a penalty zone.

Striped/Texture Fill Zone
Unexplored — Low Priority

Use a repeating diagonal stripe texture instead of a solid gradient fill. Very fine lines (1px every 4px). Creates a technical/graph-paper feel that's distinct from the smooth line.

Likely too noisy at mobile screen density. Probably not the right direction for a clean dark-mode dashboard. Listed for completeness.

FD Position — On Fill vs. Gradient (Updated)

Why Marcus is right and why the Fill is stronger than I first argued.

FD's Scorecard #1 critique treated the fill as a static colored region occupying the low-score zone. That framing was wrong. The fill area is not fixed — it grows as the line climbs. When Kai's score was 47% on his first test, there is almost no fill visible. By Practice 3 at 88%, the fill covers roughly 70% of the chart height. The chart is accumulating visual mass in proportion to his progress.

This is exactly what a progress indicator should do. The gradient variant has a clever encoding in the line stroke itself, but it is a single-channel signal. The fill variant stacks three channels: (1) the line's vertical position, (2) the area under the curve, and (3) with the new diagonal gradient, the fill brightness. All three move in the same direction — up and to the right — as Kai improves.

The remaining counterargument is that the fill technically does extend into the "below A" zone for most of the chart. But this is true of any area chart that shows progress from a low starting point. The narrative is not "this area represents failure" — it is "this is how much ground Kai has covered." Those are different readings, and the dark card background, the green A line, and the score labels all work together to make the second reading dominant.
