Design Comparison — Pros & Cons
B
Gradient Stroke
FD Pick
Pros
  • Line itself IS the narrative — gray start fades to bright white as score climbs. No legend needed to understand it.
  • No fill competing with the RIB dashed track. Two data series read clearly as separate signals.
  • Dots echo the gradient (gray → mid → bright → brightest). The dot series reinforces the story the line tells.
  • Cleaner — negative space is working. Card doesn't feel visually heavy.
  • The "dim start, bright finish" metaphor maps to Kai's actual learning arc. It's not just decoration, it has meaning.
Cons
  • Slightly less immediately readable as a trend chart. New user might not register the gradient is meaningful on first glance.
  • Starting dot is quite gray (#6E7681 stroke) — can look disconnected from the rest of the line.
  • The 88% and 95% score labels on the right crowd together more visibly when there's no fill to anchor their position.
A
Fill
Secondary
Pros
  • Conventional area chart format — immediately readable as a trend even without explanation.
  • Fill creates visual weight below the line, giving the chart a sense of grounded completeness.
  • The uniform white line on fill feels clean and high-contrast against the dark area.
Cons
  • Fill is semantically backwards for this chart: the filled zone is below the line (low scores). Eye gets drawn DOWN toward failure, not UP toward the A grade.
  • Fill competes with RIB dashed track — when both overlap in the upper range, the signal blurs.
  • Uniform line loses expressiveness — it tells you "this happened" but not "this is getting better."
  • The fill gradient (top = 14% opacity, bottom = 0%) is subtle enough that it doesn't add much, but busy enough that it dilutes the clean card feel.

Remaining Issues — New Findings
High
88% score label on right side is too close to the FA endpoint dot
The FA line endpoint (Practice 3 dot at cy=33) and the "88%" text at y=41 are only 8 SVG units apart. On a 120px viewBox that's roughly 8px visual gap. The label reads ambiguously — is it labeling the FA dot or the A line? Needs to either move further right or be offset more clearly.
Fix: push score labels to x=560 and add a 2px wide colored indicator bar (green for 95%, --text-2 for 88%) between dot and label.
High
B/C grade lines are too invisible — below threshold for functional use
The C line at 6% opacity and B line at 8% opacity are so faint they barely register on the chart. In B (Gradient) especially, with no fill to anchor the chart, the grade context ladder feels absent. User cannot orient to the scale. The A line is correctly green and visible — B and C need to step up slightly to be actually legible as reference lines.
Fix: raise B line to 14% opacity, C line to 10% opacity. Keep them recessive but make them visible enough to read as scale markers.
Mid
Bottom of chart card has inconsistent breathing room
The x-axis labels ("1st Test", "Practice 1") sit at y=116 in a 120px viewBox, then the card has 14px bottom padding on top of that. This creates roughly 14px of whitespace below the x labels. That feels okay but not locked — comparing against the 18px top padding of the chart card, the bottom feels slightly tight.
Fix: add 4px to trend card bottom padding → 18px 18px 18px. Makes top/bottom internal padding equal.
Mid
Practice 2 "Below A" badge color (blue) vs. A on Retake (green) — visual imbalance
The blue badge for "Below A" carries implicit meaning (links, actions) in dark UI design systems. It looks like a tappable pill. The green badge for "A on Retake" correctly reads as achievement. The blue badge for failure/neutral state should be gray (--text-2 tinted) rather than blue to avoid the affordance confusion.
Fix: Change badge-blu to use --text-3 text and a dark neutral background instead of the blue tint.
Mid
First attempt dot at Practice 1 (x=213) — muted RIB dot overlaps
At x=213 there are two dots: the FA solid dot (cy=74.2) and the RIB muted dot (cy=34.6). These are well-separated vertically, but in a compact viewport the area between them could confuse what the dashed line represents. A subtle connector or clearer spatial separation would help.
Note: This is a data density issue. Lower priority — acceptable in v1 if the legend is clear.
Low
tc-head margin-bottom still at 18px — test cards feel tall
The header section of each test card (name + date on left, badge on right) has 18px bottom margin before the score number. This pushes the score lower than it needs to be. 12px would keep the card compact without cramping.
Fix: .tc-head margin-bottom: 18px → 12px
Low
Hero card em dash — renders as literal "—" in title
"Practice Test #4 — Quadratics" — the em dash renders correctly but violates the Forge em-dash ban. Should be changed to a colon or period separator.
Fix: "Practice Test #4: Quadratics" or "Practice Test #4 · Quadratics"
FD Verdict

Carry B (Gradient Stroke) forward as the canonical design.

The gradient line is not decoration — it is the data. The line starts gray when Kai is at 47% and brightens to near-white as he hits 95% on Run It Back. That visual language encodes progress directly in the shape of the data itself. You don't need to read a legend to understand it — the line tells you something is getting better as it brightens.

A (Fill) is more conventional, which is also its flaw in this context. The filled zone is the "low score" zone. Every area chart that fills downward visually celebrates the wrong territory. Here, Kai's story is about climbing. The gradient stroke version points up and brightens as it goes. A is cleaner engineering — B is better design.

The high-priority fixes (score label ambiguity, B/C line opacity, badge color) are worth knocking out before this is called final. Then it's done.
