Q1b S1 polynomial multiplication 0.5 / 1
Given \(f(x)=6x^3-16x^2+2\) and \(g(x)=x^2-3x+1\), compute \(f(x)\cdot g(x)\) and write in standard form.
Kai's Answer
\(6x^5 - 34x^4 + 54x^3 - 16x^2\)
Correct Answer
\(6x^5 - 34x^4 + 54x^3 - 14x^2 - 6x + 2\)
Forgot to distribute the constant term
Kai distributed \(6x^3\) and \(-16x^2\) through \(g(x)\) but completely skipped the \(+2\) term. The missing piece: \(2(x^2-3x+1)=2x^2-6x+2\). That accounts for the three missing terms. When multiplying polynomials, every term of the first must hit every term of the second — easy to lose a constant at the end.

Q5 S2 write polynomial from zeros 0.5 / 1
Write a polynomial with x-intercepts \(-3,\; 1,\; 5\) that passes through \((-1,-3)\) and \((3,3)\).
Kai's Answer
\((x-3)(x+1)(x-5)\)
Correct Answer
\(f(x) = -\tfrac{1}{8}(x+3)(x-1)(x-5)\)
Sign errors on two factors + missing leading coefficient
Two problems here. First, Kai reversed the signs: wrote \((x-3)\) for the root \(x=-3\) and \((x+1)\) for the root \(x=1\). Remember: root \(r\) → factor \((x - r)\), so root \(-3\) → \((x-(-3))=(x+3)\), and root \(1\) → \((x-1)\). Second, Kai never found the leading coefficient \(c\). Plug a known point like \((-1,-3)\) into \(c(x+3)(x-1)(x-5)\) and solve: \(c(2)(-2)(-6)=24c=-3\), so \(c=-\tfrac{1}{8}\).
