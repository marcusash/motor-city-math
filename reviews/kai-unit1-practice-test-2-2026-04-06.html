Misread graph zeros + omitted multiplicities
The graph has dots at \(x = -3\), \(x = 1\), and \(x = 4\). Kai read the bounce at \(x=1\) as \(x=0\) and the crossing at \(x=4\) as \(x=3\) -- off by one on both. Read the gridlines carefully: the dots sit on the integer gridlines, not between them.

He also omitted all multiplicities. The curve bounces (touches and turns) at \(x=1\), which means multiplicity 2. The curve crosses at \(x=-3\) and \(x=4\), which means multiplicity 1 each. Bounce = even multiplicity. Cross = odd multiplicity. Always state them.

Q10 exponent rules like terms 0 / 1
Simplify: \((-3x^2y)(4xy^2) + (2x^3y^3) + (x^2y)(5xy^2)\)
Kai's Answer
\(2x^4y^2(-6x + 5)\)
Correct Answer
\(-5x^3y^3\)
Exponent rules + combining like terms
Two errors here. First, the exponent rule: when multiplying, add exponents on the same base.
\((-3x^2y)(4xy^2) = -12 \cdot x^{2+1} \cdot y^{1+2} = -12x^3y^3\)
\((x^2y)(5xy^2) = 5 \cdot x^{2+1} \cdot y^{1+2} = 5x^3y^3\)

All three terms are \(x^3y^3\), so they are like terms that combine:
\(-12x^3y^3 + 2x^3y^3 + 5x^3y^3 = (-12 + 2 + 5)x^3y^3 = -5x^3y^3\)

Kai got \(x^5y^2\) instead of \(x^3y^3\) (multiplied exponents instead of adding them), then tried to factor instead of combining like terms.
