Dominant Error Pattern — Study This First
Illegal Term-by-Term Cancellation
Affected questions: Q6c, Q7, Q8, Q13 — 4 of the 9 points lost.

Cancelling individual terms (like \(x\) or \(x^2\)) from numerator and denominator instead of factoring first, then cancelling common factors. Ironic: Q1 tests this exact concept and Kai answered it correctly — but repeated the mistake himself on 4 other questions. Rule: only cancel factors of the entire expression, never individual terms.

Q2 S5.d factor-simplify difference of squares 0 / 1
Simplify \(\dfrac{x^2+x}{x^2-1}\).
Kai's Answer
\(\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}\)
Correct Answer
\(\dfrac{x}{x-1}\)
Didn't factor denominator as difference of squares
Kai factored the numerator as \(x(x+1)\) but dropped the \(x\), and didn't recognize \(x^2-1\) as a difference of squares \((x+1)(x-1)\). The pattern \(a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)\) applies even when there's no middle \(x\) term — that's the hallmark of difference of squares. Factor both top and bottom completely, then cancel the common \((x+1)\) factor.
Q5 S5.d expand-simplify factoring from numerator 0 / 1
Simplify \(\dfrac{3(2x+1)^2 - 6x - 3}{(2x+1)^3}\).
Kai's Answer
\(\dfrac{6x}{2x+1}\)
Correct Answer
\(\dfrac{6x}{(2x+1)^2}\)
Didn't properly factor (2x+1) from numerator
The numerator expands to \(3(4x^2+4x+1)-6x-3 = 12x^2+12x+3-6x-3 = 12x^2+6x = 6x(2x+1)\). That single \((2x+1)\) cancels one factor from \((2x+1)^3\), leaving \((2x+1)^2\) in the denominator. Kai got the numerator right but cancelled one too many \((2x+1)\) factors. Track the exponent: \((2x+1)^3\) minus one cancelled gives \((2x+1)^2\), not \((2x+1)^1\).
Q6c S4.b add fractions illegal cancellation 0 / 1
Let \(f(x)=\dfrac{3}{12x^4}\) and \(g(x)=\dfrac{2x}{4x^3}\). Find \(f(x)+g(x)\).
Kai's Answer
\(\dfrac{3+6x^2}{12x^4}\)
Correct Answer
\(\dfrac{2x^2+1}{4x^4}\)
Illegal cancellation — x² not a factor of every term
Kai correctly found the LCD and combined: \(\frac{3+6x^2}{12x^4}\). That's a valid unsimplified form. But then tried to cancel \(x^2\) from the numerator and denominator. You can only cancel factors of the entire numerator, not individual terms. The correct simplification: factor 3 from the top: \(\frac{3(1+2x^2)}{12x^4} = \frac{2x^2+1}{4x^4}\).

Q7 S4.a multiply-simplify illegal cancellation 0 / 1

Kai's Answer
\(\dfrac{3x}{3x+5} = \dfrac{3}{8}\)
Correct Answer
\(\dfrac{3x}{3x+5}\)
Illegal cancel — x is not a factor of (3x+5)

Q10 S4.b subtract-rational LCD 0 / 1
Subtract and simplify by making the least common denominator: \(\dfrac{x}{x+1} - \dfrac{x^2-3x-5}{x^2+5x+4}\).
Kai's Answer
Blank — no answer submitted.
Correct Answer
\(\dfrac{7x+5}{(x+1)(x+4)}\)
No answer submitted
Kai left this blank on the test. The key skill: factor the more complex denominator to find the LCD, rewrite each fraction, then combine. Step by step: factor \(x^2+5x+4=(x+1)(x+4)\), so the LCD is \((x+1)(x+4)\). Rewrite: \(\frac{x(x+4)}{(x+1)(x+4)} - \frac{x^2-3x-5}{(x+1)(x+4)} = \frac{x^2+4x-x^2+3x+5}{(x+1)(x+4)} = \frac{7x+5}{(x+1)(x+4)}\). Watch the minus sign — distribute it across every term of the second numerator.
Q11 S4.b add-rational complex fraction 0 / 1
If \(x > 3\), which of the following is equivalent to \(\dfrac{1}{\frac{1}{x+2}+\frac{1}{x+3}}\)?
Kai's Answer
E) None of the above
Correct Answer
B) \(\dfrac{x^2+5x+6}{2x+5}\)
Didn't simplify the complex fraction
This is a complex fraction. First add the fractions in the denominator: \(\frac{1}{x+2}+\frac{1}{x+3}=\frac{2x+5}{(x+2)(x+3)}\). Then flip: \(\frac{1}{\frac{2x+5}{(x+2)(x+3)}} = \frac{(x+2)(x+3)}{2x+5} = \frac{x^2+5x+6}{2x+5}\). That's choice B.
Q13 S5.d simplify-quotient term-by-term cancellation 0 / 1
If \(h(x) = x^3 - 9x\) and \(k(x) = x^2 - 2x - 3\), write a simplified expression for \(\dfrac{h(x)}{k(x)}\).
Kai's Answer
\(\dfrac{-8x}{-2x-3}\)
Correct Answer
\(\dfrac{x(x+3)}{x+1}\)
Critical: term-by-term cancellation instead of factoring
Kai subtracted \(x^2\) from numerator and denominator as if cancelling: \(x^3-9x \to -8x\) and \(x^2-2x-3 \to -2x-3\). This is not how simplification works. You must FACTOR first, then cancel common factors. Correct: \(\frac{x(x+3)(x-3)}{(x-3)(x+1)}\), cancel \((x-3)\), giving \(\frac{x(x+3)}{x+1}\). Never subtract or remove terms from a fraction — only cancel factors.
Coaching Note
This is the same conceptual error tested in Q1 (Kyeung-min's mistake). Kai correctly identified the error in Q1 but made it himself in Q13, Q7, Q8, and Q6c.
Q14 other difference quotient expansion error 0 / 1
Simplify: \(\dfrac{2(x+h)^2 - 3(x+h) - (2x^2 - 3x)}{h}\).
Kai's Answer
\(4x^2 - 2x + 2h - 3\)
Correct Answer
\(4x + 2h - 3\)
Expansion error in 2(x+h)²
Expand step by step: \((x+h)^2 = x^2+2xh+h^2\), then \(2(x+h)^2 = 2x^2+4xh+2h^2\). Subtract \(3(x+h) = 3x+3h\). Subtract \((2x^2-3x)\). The \(2x^2\) and \(-2x^2\) cancel, the \(-3x\) and \(+3x\) cancel, leaving \(4xh+2h^2-3h\). Factor out \(h\): \(h(4x+2h-3)\). Divide by \(h\): \(4x+2h-3\). Kai's \(x^2\) and \(x\) terms should have cancelled but didn't — the expansion of \(2(x+h)^2\) went wrong.
